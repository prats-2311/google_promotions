# Agentic Tour & Promotion Intelligence OS

A detailed concept document for a hackathon project based on a multi-agent AI system that helps actors, directors, producers, singers, musicians, and touring artists make each city stop more culturally aligned, locally resonant, and emotionally memorable for fans.

## Overview

This project proposes a functional, production-style AI agent or multi-agent system for the entertainment and media industry that focuses on a major but underexplored stage of the value chain: post-production promotion, press tours, fan engagement, and global performance planning. The hackathon brief explicitly asks participants to build a functional, production-ready AI agent or multi-agent network that solves critical bottlenecks across the entertainment and media value chain for filmmakers, studio crews, screenwriters, or fans. It also frames strong submissions around autonomous agent networks, managed production-style pipelines, and governance-oriented systems.[1]

The proposed product addresses a real operational problem: when actors, directors, producers, or musicians travel across continents, countries, and cities to promote a movie, TV show, or live performance, each audience responds differently. Cultural expectations differ. Media behavior differs. Audience enthusiasm differs. Small localized gestures can create disproportionate fan response, but most campaigns still rely on generic global messaging with light local tweaks. The system aims to turn that guesswork into structured intelligence and actionable city-by-city briefs.

## Core Idea

The project can be framed as an agentic platform that prepares entertainment teams before they arrive in a city. For a film team, it helps optimize a press tour. For a singer or touring musician, it helps make every concert stop feel unique and locally loved. The central insight is simple: fans respond strongly when artists demonstrate local awareness, linguistic effort, cultural respect, and city-specific personalization.

Examples discussed during ideation included how audiences in India often respond enthusiastically when global stars speak Hindi, reference local icons, or perform culturally relevant moments during tours and promotions. Those moments can make a visit feel memorable rather than generic. The system is designed to help plan those moments intentionally instead of leaving them to improvisation.

## Problem Statement

Entertainment promotion is often highly visible but poorly systematized. Studios, labels, PR teams, and artist managers must make campaign decisions across many cities with limited local intelligence and limited time. This creates several recurring problems:

- Teams do not always know what local audiences care about most.
- They may not understand local etiquette, media expectations, or fan culture.
- They may miss simple, high-impact moments such as greeting fans in the local language or referencing beloved local cultural icons.
- They may over-invest in the wrong cities or under-invest in cities with high enthusiasm potential.
- They often run a generic tour template instead of a city-sensitive promotional experience.

The result is a missed opportunity in fan engagement, weaker event turnout, flatter emotional response, and less memorable promotion.

This idea maps well to the hackathon because it still sits clearly inside the entertainment and media value chain, but it explores a less saturated problem area than the usual script-writing or editing assistants. The challenge page emphasizes solving critical bottlenecks across the value chain and highlights not only filmmakers and studio teams but also fans as a key audience.[1]

This concept is strong for several reasons:

- It is naturally multi-agent rather than being a thin chatbot wrapper.

The idea is also non-obvious. Many hackathon teams are likely to build writing assistants, editing copilots, or production management dashboards. A tour and promotion intelligence platform stands out because it focuses on cultural localization, city-specific engagement, and fan delight across global entertainment campaigns.

## Feasibility Assessment

The idea is feasible as a hackathon MVP if it is intentionally constrained. A realistic prototype does not need live global intelligence across every city on earth. Instead, it needs a narrow but convincing workflow that demonstrates how the system would work in practice.

A feasible MVP would:

- Focus on one primary narrative, such as a fictional film promotion tour or a fictional world concert tour.
- Cover around 4 to 6 cities only.
- Use a small number of specialized agents.
- Produce structured outputs such as city briefs, local delight suggestions, and itinerary recommendations.
- Present everything through a polished dashboard rather than a basic chat interface.

The idea becomes less feasible if it tries to support every entertainment format, every city, real-time live social data ingestion, and enterprise-grade analytics all at once.

## Target Users

The core users discussed for the system are:

- Actors, directors, and producers going on press tours.
- Studio marketing and PR teams planning multi-city campaigns.
- Local distribution or event teams needing localization guidance.
- Singers, musicians, bands, and touring artists.
- Artist managers and label promotion teams.

The project serves both sides of the value chain: internal entertainment teams and fan-facing experiences. That matches the challenge direction, which includes both creators and fans as important stakeholders.[1]

## Primary Use Cases

### Film and TV Promotion
After a movie or network/streaming show is completed, cast and creators travel to promote it. They need city-by-city knowledge about local audience taste, cultural context, language sensitivities, media expectations, and possible fan engagement tactics.

### Music Tours and Live Performances
Artists often travel globally, and fans especially love city-specific moments such as local language greetings, local pop culture callbacks, remixed local hits, references to beloved actors or songs, and interactive crowd moments. The system can help make these touches intentional and repeatable.

### Talent Preparation
Before appearing in a city, actors, directors, or musicians can receive a short brief with local phrases, etiquette guidance, themes to mention, topics to avoid, and suggested cultural references.

### Campaign Planning
Marketing teams can compare cities, rank enthusiasm potential, plan different event formats, and allocate attention to the markets with the highest strategic value.

## Core Insight: City-Specific Moments Matter

A central theme of the idea is that local gestures can create disproportionate emotional impact. Fans often respond intensely when global stars demonstrate awareness of their language, icons, references, or traditions. A generic city stop can become a legendary experience if the artist says a few words in the local language, references a beloved local figure, or adapts the performance or promotional format to local expectations.

This matters because it connects directly to turnout, online buzz, fan memory, and emotional loyalty. The product therefore does not only optimize logistics; it optimizes resonance.

## Product Vision

The long-term product vision is an intelligent platform that makes every tour stop feel informed, local, and intentional. Rather than giving teams a generic campaign template, it generates tailored city-level intelligence and actionable creative guidance.

A finished product could help users answer questions such as:

- What should this actor mention in Mumbai to create instant audience connection?
- Which three cities deserve the biggest fan event investment for this campaign?
- What local language lines are safe, useful, and memorable for the artist to say on stage?
- What kind of crowd interaction style works best in this city?
- What local cultural references would feel delightful rather than forced?

### Measurable Impact

Beyond emotional resonance, the product should make one concrete, quantified operational claim rather than relying on qualitative language alone. The target claim for this MVP: reducing per-city promotional brief preparation from an estimated several hours of manual research per stop (gathering cultural notes, phrasing, and talking points by hand) to minutes, generated on demand from the curated city Data Store. This is the answer to the "measurable operational outcomes" bar found in partner-track judging criteria, and should be stated explicitly in the demo narration rather than left implicit.

## Multi-Agent System Design

The idea works best as a multi-agent workflow. That matters because the hackathon specifically emphasizes autonomous agent networks and production-oriented orchestration rather than simple prompting.[1]

**MVP scope note:** Six agent roles are described below, but the MVP builds five — Culture Intelligence, Fan Enthusiasm, Local Delight, Talent Prep, and the Campaign Orchestrator. The Promotion Architect Agent (campaign-format recommendations) is explicitly Phase 2: it depends on the other four already being reliable, and campaign-format advice is less central to the signature "city delight card" demo than the four agents that feed it directly. This resolves the earlier inconsistency between listing six agents and recommending "three to five agent roles" for MVP.

**Making this a real network, not just a pipeline:** a system where the Orchestrator calls agents in strict sequence is a pipeline, not a network — and the hackathon explicitly asks for agent networks. To earn that framing honestly, the Talent Prep Agent does not simply consume the Culture Intelligence Agent's output once. Before finalizing a brief, it sends its draft back to the Culture Intelligence Agent for a grounding check (does this draft contradict any do/don't guidance?). This also directly satisfies the hackathon's "remain grounded, no hallucination" requirement,[1] rather than that requirement being addressed only in prose.

### 1. Culture Intelligence Agent
Responsible for summarizing cultural norms, etiquette, interaction style expectations, local communication tone, humor boundaries, and sensitive areas.

Outputs may include:
- Do and do not guidance.
- Greeting style recommendations.
- Media behavior expectations.
- Fan interaction style notes.

### 2. Fan Enthusiasm Agent
Responsible for estimating the likely heat level of a city or audience segment for a title, genre, artist, or campaign type.

Outputs may include:
- Enthusiasm score.
- Fan behavior style.
- City importance tier.
- Genre affinity summary.

### 3. Local Delight Agent
This is one of the most distinctive parts of the product. It suggests city-specific emotional hooks.

Outputs may include:
- Local language phrases to say.
- Local songs, remix possibilities, or cultural references.
- Beloved local stars or iconic references worth mentioning.
- Suggestions for memorable audience moments.

### 4. Promotion Architect Agent
Responsible for deciding what kind of campaign actions make sense for each city.

Outputs may include:
- Event format recommendations.
- Schedule structure suggestions.
- Press vs fan-event balance.
- Talk track and promotion angle.

### 5. Talent Prep Agent
Responsible for transforming analysis into a concise and human-usable brief for the actor, director, producer, or musician. Before the brief is marked final, it sends its draft back to the Culture Intelligence Agent for a grounding check, so the two agents form a real two-way handoff rather than a one-directional pipeline step.

Outputs may include:
- Pre-event briefing card.
- Topics to lean into.
- Topics to avoid.
- Pronounceable local lines.
- High-probability fan questions.

### 6. Campaign Orchestrator
This coordinating layer receives the campaign input and triggers the right agents in sequence. It collects outputs and transforms them into final artifacts inside the dashboard.

## Suggested Workflow

A possible user workflow could be:

1. The user creates a campaign for a film, show, or tour.
2. The user selects cities and dates.
3. The orchestration system triggers the core agents.
4. Each city receives a generated profile.
5. The platform outputs a city summary, enthusiasm score, local delight opportunities, and recommended event strategy.
6. The user can open a talent briefing card for each stop.
7. The campaign team can compare cities across the full route.

This is a clean workflow for a judge to understand in a short demo.

## Sample City Delight Card Structure

A city delight card could include:

- City name and campaign stop date.
- Audience energy summary.
- Three short local language phrases.
- One or two high-confidence cultural hooks.
- Suggested local icon or media reference.
- Recommended stage or interview tone.
- Crowd interaction suggestion.
- Avoid list.

For example, a Mumbai card might emphasize Hindi phrases, Bollywood sensitivity, high-energy crowd participation, and the value of a respectful Shah Rukh Khan or iconic-song reference if contextually appropriate.

## Risks and Honest Weaknesses

The concept is strong, but several risks must be acknowledged.

### 1. Scope Creep
The idea can easily expand into a giant global marketing platform. That is dangerous in a hackathon.

### 2. Data Realism
The platform may not have access to rich real-time verified city-level fan data during the prototype. This means the enthusiasm model should be presented honestly as an intelligent planning layer rather than a definitive forecasting engine.

### 3. Generic AI Output Risk
If the generated city recommendations sound shallow or generic, the product will lose credibility. The UX must structure outputs cleanly and the prompts or rules must be tuned carefully.

### 4. Confused Persona Risk
If the product tries to serve actors, PR teams, labels, studios, and event organizers equally in the MVP, the result may feel blurry. The MVP should clearly prioritize one primary persona while remaining extensible.

## Recommendation on Persona

The best primary persona for the MVP is likely the marketing and promotion team, because that makes the dashboard structure easier to justify. Talent-facing briefing cards can still exist as a key output artifact.

This gives the product a coherent structure:
- Main dashboard for campaign planners.
- City cards for planning.
- Talent brief as exportable output.

## Recommendation on Scope

To maximize the chance of a strong submission, the project should:

- Anchor around a film promo tour as the main storyline.
- Mention musicians and touring artists as a secondary extension.
- Limit to a small set of cities.
- Build visible agent orchestration.
- Focus on one exceptional signature experience: the city delight system.

## Technical Implementation Direction

The system is built entirely on Google Cloud infrastructure, per the hackathon's required stack.[1] Partner-track requirements (for example, IBM's mandatory use of IBM Bob as a development tool) are intentionally deferred — the core architecture below has no dependency on any partner tool, so partner compliance can be layered on right before submission without any redesign.

Implementation structure:

- **Gemini** as the reasoning model behind every agent (Culture Intelligence, Fan Enthusiasm, Local Delight, Talent Prep, Orchestrator).
- **Google Cloud Agent Builder (Playbooks)** as the orchestration layer — defines each agent's role and the handoff sequence, replacing any custom Node/Next.js backend.
- **BigQuery holds curated per-city reference tables** (etiquette notes, local phrases, fan-behavior signals, cultural references) for the 5 demo cities — a deliberate, honestly-curated dataset, not live ingestion (see "Data Realism" risk below). **Superseded:** the original plan to expose this via a native Agent Builder "Data Store" was dropped after research confirmed Data Stores (even the BigQuery-backed "structured" type) are semantic/RAG search — embedding + LLM summarization, no guarantee of an exact row by key, and excluded from the Dialogflow CX SLA. Instead, a small Cloud Run service (`tour_data_api`) runs real parameterized SQL and is exposed to Playbooks as an OpenAPI Tool — deterministic, and a stronger "no hallucination" story for judges. This is live and verified end-to-end as of 2026-07-28.
- **Enterprise Agent Platform SDK (Python)** for logic Playbooks can't express directly — notably the enthusiasm-scoring calculation and city ranking/comparison logic.
- **Cloud Run** hosting the dashboard and any custom integration endpoints.
- **Secret Manager** for any credentials if external non-Google sources are added later.

### Making the agents action-driven, not just chat

The hackathon's Production Goals explicitly require agents that act — executing tool calls, updating dynamic databases, and triggering cloud functions — rather than only generating text.[1] To satisfy this concretely rather than only in prose:

- The **Campaign Orchestrator** writes each finalized city brief as a real record into the BigQuery-backed Data Store (an actual database update, not just a returned string).
- The **Talent Prep Agent** triggers a Cloud Run function to render the final city delight card as an exportable artifact once its draft passes the grounding check described below.
- A stretch goal (not required for MVP) is letting the Fan Enthusiasm Agent pull one live public signal (for example, a public event-listing or search API) for a single demo city, to show the architecture can extend beyond seeded data.

The submission page requires a project URL, a public code repository with a license file, and a three-minute demo video, so the product should be engineered to produce a polished, easily recordable user flow.[1]

## Final Verdict

This is a strong, competitive hackathon idea with clear emotional appeal, a solid multi-agent structure, and strong demo potential. Its strength lies in transforming a widely recognized but loosely managed problem into a structured and visually compelling AI workflow product. The idea is feasible if it is narrowly scoped, disciplined in execution, and centered on a single compelling narrative.

The concept should be built as a focused MVP rather than a universal platform. If executed with a polished dashboard, visible agent orchestration, and one memorable city-specific example, it has the potential to stand out as both creative and practical.
